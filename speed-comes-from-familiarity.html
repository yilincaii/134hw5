Speed Comes From Familiarity, Not Rushing

2026 · Algorithms

I used to think getting faster at LeetCode meant forcing myself to finish more questions in less time. Most of the time, that only made me read too quickly and miss important details.

After practicing the same topic for several days, I noticed that speed came naturally. I became faster because I had seen the structure before, not because I was typing faster.

For example, when I see a substring problem now, I check whether the window needs to grow and shrink. When I see a tree problem, I ask what information should return from each recursive call.

This also changed how I measure progress. Solving ten random questions is not always better than spending two days understanding one pattern really well.

My current goal is to practice fewer topics at one time and revisit similar questions until the basic approach feels natural. I would rather understand fifty useful problems deeply than rush through five hundred and forget most of them.
