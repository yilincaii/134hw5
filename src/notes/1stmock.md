I recently practiced a mock technical interview with Zion (a very nice Google SDE!!), and one thing became very clear: knowing the interview process is not the same as being able to perform it naturally under pressure.

I already knew that I should discuss time and space complexity, but during the interview, I still needed prompts before bringing them up. That showed me this cannot remain something I &ldquo;remember eventually.&rdquo; It needs to become automatic. My goal now is to build the habit of stating complexity without waiting for the interviewer to ask.

I also used to worry that asking too many clarification questions might make me look unprepared. Zion helped me see the opposite. Making assumptions without checking can be a much worse signal. Even for something small, like whether I can use Python&rsquo;s built-in `max()` function, it is better to ask:

> Would you like me to use the built-in function, or implement it manually?

That kind of question shows awareness, not weakness.

My biggest takeaway is that strong interview communication is not about speaking constantly. It is about noticing the details that need confirmation and raising them at the right time.

Thank you again to Zion for the thoughtful mock interview! My next step is to turn these habits into *muscle memory.*
